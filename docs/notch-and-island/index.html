On a MacBook with a notch, Warashi puts two numbers right beside it: CPU on the left and memory on the right by default. On displays without a notch, such as an external monitor, a small black island sits where a notch would be.

Open the mini dashboard

  1. Rest the pointer on the notch

    Hover the notch (or the island) for a moment. It swells a little, then opens downward into a live dashboard: by default CPU, power and memory with the last minute, and the three busiest apps. You can change all of it.

  2. Move away to close it

    Move the pointer away and it closes. Click it to open the Warashi window.

Turn them on or off, and choose the numbers

  1. Open Settings › Notch

    Press ⌘, in the popover or window and choose the Notch tab.

  2. Switch each one

    Glance beside the notch controls the numbers on this MacBook; Island on displays without a notch controls the pill on other screens. Off removes them completely.

  3. Pick the numbers

    At the top of the tab, click either number beside the notch to choose what that side shows, or press the arrows between them to swap sides.

Choose what the open notch shows

  1. Open the editor

    Click the sliders button in the open notch, or go to Settings › Notch. You see the open notch itself, live.

  2. Change it

    Press − or +, and drag anything to move it. There's room for up to six readings, plus the top apps and your connected devices. The full walk-through is in Arrange the popover & notch.

Good to know: When an alert fires, the notch opens by itself for a few seconds with the reason, and its edge lights up. A pointer left resting on the notch closes it after ten seconds, to keep Warashi light.

If something's not right

  • The island covers something on your external display: turn off Island on displays without a notch.
