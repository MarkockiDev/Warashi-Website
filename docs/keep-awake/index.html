Keep Awake

Keep Awake stops your Mac from going to sleep while you need it: a long download, a build, a presentation, a video call.

  1. Turn it on

    Click Awake in the menu bar window or the notch: your Mac stays awake until you turn it off. Press and hold Awake, or use Start on the Keep Awake tile in Tools, for more choices.

  2. Choose for how long

    Until I Turn It Off, For 1 Hour, For 2 Hours, For 4 Hours, Until a Time, Until the Work Is Done or While an App Is Open. Timed sessions can be extended with Add an Hour.

  3. Turn it off

    Click Awake again, or choose Turn Off.

The choices, explained

  • Until the Work Is Done stays awake while your Mac is busy (CPU at 20% or more, or a download or upload running) and lets it sleep after three quiet minutes. If nothing gets busy within five minutes, it stops.
  • While an App Is Open keeps your Mac awake until the app you choose quits.
  • Keep the Screen On also stops the display sleeping. It's off by default, so the screen can still dim.
  • Automatically can turn Keep Awake on by itself When Plugged In or When a Display Is Connected.
  • Hot Key (⌃⌥⌘K) turns Keep Awake on and off from any app.

With the lid closed

Stay Awake With the Lid Closed lets a MacBook keep working with its lid shut, for example while an AI agent or a build runs. It needs the charger and the System helper, and it's for this session only. For your Mac's safety it turns off by itself if you unplug, if macOS says the Mac is too hot, after 12 hours, or if Warashi stops answering.

Good to know: Keep Awake also stops when the battery reaches 10% unplugged, and whenever Warashi quits: it never keeps your Mac awake behind your back. Sessions of a minute or more are noted in the Flight Log.
