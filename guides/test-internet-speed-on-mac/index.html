How to test your internet speed on a Mac

Measure your connection with the tool built into macOS, understand the numbers, and get a result you can trust.

3 min read · Updated 9 November 2026

Your Mac has a good speed test built in, made by Apple. Here's how to use it, and what the results actually mean.

1. Run Apple's test

Open Terminal (Applications › Utilities), type networkQuality and press Return. It takes about a minute and then prints your upload and download capacity and your connection's responsiveness.

2. What the numbers mean

  • Download and upload are how much your connection can move each second, in megabits. Download matters for streaming and web pages; upload matters for video calls and backups.
  • Responsiveness is how many round trips your connection manages in a minute while it's busy. Higher is better. A fast connection with low responsiveness is why a video call can stutter while someone else downloads.
  • Idle latency is how long a reply takes when nothing else is going on.

3. Get a result you can trust

  • Pause big downloads, cloud backups and streaming first.
  • A VPN usually lowers the result, so turn it off to test the connection itself.
  • On Wi-Fi, test close to your router, and then from where you usually sit: the difference tells you whether the problem is your Wi-Fi or your internet.
  • Run it two or three times. One result is only a moment.

4. Mind your data

A speed test moves real data, which can reach 2 GB or more on a fast connection. Avoid running it on a phone hotspot or a capped plan.
