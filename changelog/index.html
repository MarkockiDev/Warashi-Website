ChangelogWhat's new

Every version, in plain words.

Pre-release versions, while Warashi gets ready for launch.

0.3.65
26 Nov 2026
  • Four more languages. Warashi now speaks Italian, Dutch, Indonesian and Arabic, in the app and on this website, for eleven languages in all. Arabic runs right to left, mirrored throughout. Choose one in Settings › General › Language, or let Warashi follow your Mac.
  • Smaller fixes. The break’s Now button in the notch reads right in Japanese, and the notch’s summary line keeps each number with its unit.
0.3.64
26 Nov 2026
  • The notch, rethought. When nothing’s going on, the notch is just the notch. When something is (an agent at work or waiting for you, a break coming up, Keep Awake, charging, a speed test, Soft Focus), the most important sits left of the notch and the next right of it. Point at it and each opens as a row with its controls: Open the agent’s app, Skip a break, Add an Hour, Stop. Prefer two numbers beside a calm notch? Turn on Readings when nothing’s going on in Settings › Customize.
  • Notices come from the notch. With the notch or island on, alerts, the low-memory and Trash cards, and your coding agents’ notes (done, needs you, near a limit) come from the notch instead of a notification. Each says what happened in plain words, with Tell Me More for the page that explains it; an app using a lot of CPU or memory can be quit right there. Then the notice waits beside the notch until you deal with it.
  • Keep Awake says why it’s on, such as “while an agent works”, so it never seems to switch on by itself.
  • Allow… on the Claude Code and Cursor cards when macOS needs your OK to read their sign-in, so their limits show again.
  • Polish throughout. More room between the menu bar window’s cards, a welcome tour that opens at once, Settings that open faster, units on History’s headline, whole gigabytes on Now, words for a quiet Checkup week, and Polish and French text that fits.
0.3.63
23 Nov 2026
  • Clean Up. A new tab in Storage gathers what could go, in three groups by how careful to be. Safe to clean: app and browser caches, logs, Xcode’s build data and package downloads, which come back by themselves; Move All to Trash moves them together after one question. Look first: installers in Downloads over a week old, second copies of a download, and the copies Mail keeps of attachments you opened. Handle with care: iPhone and iPad backups, which may be the only copy. Caches of open apps and macOS’s own are left alone, and everything goes to the Trash, so it can be put back.
  • How much is safe to clean shows on Overview’s new Clean Up card and under Disk in the menu bar window, next to the purgeable space macOS clears by itself when it needs room.
  • The Trash card says how much emptying freed.
  • Usage alerts on from the start. Runaway CPU, memory growth, heavy memory use and heat are now on, also if you installed before; switch any off in Settings › Alerts. A problem that was already going when its alert was switched on, or came back within the hour, is now told.
  • A video holds a break for 10 minutes at most. A browser can keep the screen awake all afternoon for a muted clip; after 10 minutes the countdown shows, with Skip and +5 min.
0.3.62
21 Nov 2026
  • Heavy memory use. A new alert, like CleanMyMac’s. When macOS is short of memory (pressure critical, or high for 2 minutes) and an app you can quit holds a tenth of it or more, a card drops from the menu bar with up to three such apps and their sizes. Quit uses each app’s own quit, so it asks about unsaved work; Force Quit is offered only if it’s still open after 5 seconds. Ignore can wait, leave out an app for good, or turn the alert off. It follows macOS’s memory pressure, never how full the memory bar looks.
  • Breaks wait for calls, not dictation. A call now means a camera on, or an app both listening and speaking. Dictation apps such as Wispr Flow only listen, so a countdown no longer reaches zero and stops.
  • The break pill sits lower, clear of the notch.
  • Quick actions with room to breathe. Awake, Break, Speed, Slow? and Charge in the menu bar window now show their symbol above the word, so the row no longer feels crowded.
0.3.61
21 Nov 2026
  • The break countdown keeps going. The heads-up before a break could appear, vanish after a moment and start again from 0:15. Warashi now checks for a call or a video before it shows the countdown, and once shown the countdown runs to zero; only a call still on at that moment holds the break back.
0.3.60
21 Nov 2026
  • No more Keychain prompts out of the blue. Warashi reads Claude Code’s and Cursor’s sign-ins quietly in the background. If it isn’t allowed yet, it waits instead of showing macOS’s prompt, which kept asking again for anyone who had chosen Allow rather than Always Allow. macOS asks only when you turn on a usage limit or press Allow… in Settings › Coding Agents, which says when an OK is needed.
0.3.59
21 Nov 2026
  • A new welcome tour. Rebuilt from the ground up: nine short chapters, each showing a feature live with one thing to try, and the permission it needs right underneath, with why and one button. Notifications are asked on Alerts, Full Disk Access on Storage, the System helper on Battery, Open at login on the menu bar, and Claude Code’s sign-in on Agents; History and Breaks say they need nothing. Each permission ticks itself off the moment you allow it in System Settings.
  • Chapters that don’t apply are left out: Battery appears only on Macs with a battery, and Agents only if you use Claude Code, Codex or Cursor. Move between chapters with the dots at the top, or ← and →. The last page shows what’s on and what each permission opens.
  • To see it again: Settings › General › Show Welcome Again.
0.3.58
20 Nov 2026
  • Coding agents. Turn on Settings › Coding Agents and Warashi watches Claude Code, Codex and Cursor: what each session is doing, your 5-hour and weekly limits with when they reset, and a note in a notification and the notch when an agent needs you or has finished. The note stays until you go back to the agent’s app, and doesn’t appear if you’re already there. Keep Awake can stay on while an agent works, lid closed included, and long sessions are written in the Flight Log. Warashi reads only each session’s state and its limits, never your code, prompts or conversations.
  • Claude’s usage limit, through Claude Code’s own sign-in. Settings shows who Claude Code is signed in as, with Sign In (Claude Code’s own login, in Terminal) and Renew Now. A stale sign-in renews by itself through one tiny Claude Code request, at most every six hours. Cursor’s monthly usage shows the same way. Warashi keeps no credential of its own.
  • Discharge while plugged in. In Health › Battery or Checkup, run your Mac on its battery with the charger still connected, down to 80, 70, 60, 50, 40 or 30%, then let charging take over. It stops by itself if you unplug, the Mac sleeps or gets hot, the battery runs low or Warashi quits. Needs the System helper.
  • Your battery in the menu bar. Beside the spirit can now show a battery filled to its level, with a bolt while charging and a pause while it’s held at your charge limit. New readouts from Warashi’s features: battery time left, your coding agent’s limit, Keep Awake’s time left, the next break, and VPN with download speed.
  • Trash getting big. A new alert: when the Trash passes 1, 2, 5 or 10 GB, a card drops from the menu bar item with Empty Trash… (one confirmation) or Ignore, which waits until it grows by that much again.
  • Developer clean-up. Health › Storage › Developer finds Xcode’s build products, old simulators and device support, node_modules, package caches and Docker’s leftovers, and says whether each comes back by itself. Checkup gains Homebrew: what’s outdated and what brew cleanup would free, without going online.
  • Two new alerts: a compiler stuck at a full core for 10 minutes, named with its project and the agent that started it, with Quit Compiler; and a VPN that disconnects. The Developer page also lists the local AI models Ollama or LM Studio has loaded.
  • Keep Awake always keeps the screen on. warashi:// links turn Keep Awake and Soft Focus on or off and open any page, for Shortcuts and launchers, and warashi agents lists your agents in Terminal.
  • Every page keeps the same window size, the size of Now.
0.3.57
17 Nov 2026
  • Quit and Force Quit from Apps. Right-click an app, or open it, and choose Quit…: it closes the normal way and asks about anything unsaved. If it’s still open a few seconds later, Force Quit… appears, and asks once more. One process of an app, such as a runaway browser tab, can be quit on its own. macOS’s own processes, other users’ and Warashi itself are never offered.
  • The welcome tour is hands-on: eleven pages, now with Alerts, Breaks and Soft Focus. Every switch is the real setting and says what now works; notifications are asked for the moment you turn on your first alert, with a test to try. Each page says where to change it later and links to its Help page.
0.3.55
16 Nov 2026
  • Soft Focus, a new tool: every window except the one you’re working in softens into a blur, so only your work stays sharp. Choose Ambient or Deep, a tint and film grain, keep the whole app sharp, blur only the display you’re on, and list apps it steps aside for. Turn it on from Tools, with ⌃⌥⌘F, or, if you switch it on, a shake of the pointer. No permission: macOS does the blurring, and Warashi never sees your screen.
  • Empty Trash now empties it completely. What only an administrator can delete, such as an app an installer put in Applications, is handed to Finder, which asks for your password. macOS asks once to let Warashi control Finder, for this and nothing else.
  • Sit up straight rises from the bottom to the middle of your main display, and only there.
  • Warashi Help opens the Help pages on warashi.app, in the language Warashi uses, from the Help menu, Settings and the menu bar window.
  • With another notch app running, Warashi’s notch stays on top and answers the pointer, and Settings › Customize names the other app.
0.3.54
16 Nov 2026
  • A new welcome tour: eight short pages that show each part of Warashi with its real views, live, and how to reach it. A Permissions page offers Notifications, the System helper and Full Disk Access, each with what it opens and its live state, and each can be skipped.
  • The break screen asks plainly: “Look away from the screen.” (or, for a rest, “Step away from your Mac.”), over a slow, breathing glow of colour behind the blurred desktop. It stays still with Reduce Motion. Skip is larger, and the spirit in the corner is gone.
  • The heads-up before a break is larger, its buttons light up under the pointer and give a little when pressed, and it no longer shows a grey box behind it.
  • Checkup: the battery and storage row grows with its content, so a note about another charge manager no longer spills over the tiles; free space shows in whole gigabytes inside the ring.
0.3.53
14 Nov 2026
  • Lighter when you look: the menu bar window and the main window now redraw only the numbers that moved, so an open window costs a fraction of what it did, with the same counting numbers, sliding charts and easing rings.
  • Quieter when you don’t: while your screens sleep or the Mac is locked, Warashi stops drawing and takes a reading every 30 seconds instead of every 5.
  • Flat buttons and icons: the one primary button on each screen is solid clay, and tool icons are flat squares in their colour. Settings panes open straight on their settings.
  • Overview tiles carry their metric’s icon before the caption, in the same place on every tile; Disk and Battery centre their reading.
  • Sit up straight is a round badge with a larger figure, the words around its rim, and a slower rise from the bottom of the screen.
  • The Disk tile in the menu bar window is the Storage ring in miniature, with the same bands and colours.
  • Apps lists Warashi itself as “this window” and keeps it out of the busiest-apps rankings, so its own window never looks like a culprit.
  • Storage scans use at most half the cores, count as fresh for a day, and don’t start by themselves on battery.
  • Tools keep one fixed order; the favourites star is gone.
0.3.52
14 Nov 2026
  • Storage does more: step into any folder to see what fills it, see your Downloads oldest first with old installers marked, and find local AI models from Ollama, LM Studio and Hugging Face.
  • Remove apps properly: Storage › Apps lists every app, those you haven't opened in months first, and moves an app and what it left in your Library to the Trash, item by item, after you've seen each one. It also finds what apps you've already deleted left behind.
  • Anything you remove goes to the Trash first, so you can put it back until you empty it. Big files, downloads and your own folders can go there too.
  • Checkup: Malware protection shows how up to date XProtect is, and Check Apps checks every app and login item's signature. Local snapshots shows Time Machine's copies on the disk and can free their space. App permissions lists every privacy page in System Settings.
  • Keep Awake keeps working with the lid closed, and carries on when Warashi updates or restarts.
  • Battery: how it has aged over the months, and the Flight Log now notes when it charged past your limit or ran on battery while plugged in, naming the app that did it. A warning when another app such as AlDente also manages charging.
  • Breaks only in your work hours, paused during a Focus such as Do Not Disturb, and a switch for waiting during calls and meetings. Three new nudges: stretch, drink some water and relax your jaw.
  • Live countdowns in the notch: a running speed test, a rest coming up or a timed Keep Awake.
  • Tools: hide desktop icons for a screen share, mute the microphone in every app, and a key tester in Clean Keyboard.
  • Siri: say “Keep my Mac awake with Warashi”, “Hide desktop icons with Warashi” and more, in every language Warashi speaks.
  • Developer: running Docker containers with their CPU, memory and ports.
  • The helper checks itself and says when a restart would fix it.
0.3.51
13 Nov 2026
  • Slowdowns in the Flight Log: when your Mac struggles for 2 minutes or more, Warashi writes down how long and why, such as an app using most of the processor, memory running short, the Mac cooling off, or Spotlight indexing.
  • Three new alerts, off until you turn them on: Mac struggling, Backup overdue (Time Machine hasn't backed up for a week) and macOS update waiting (for a week).
  • Slow? in the menu bar window: one click opens Why is my Mac slow? and starts the check.
0.3.49
13 Nov 2026
  • Why is my Mac slow? In Checkup, press Feeling Slow? and Warashi watches for ten seconds, compares what it sees with your Mac's usual week, and names the likely cause, with one thing to do.
  • Security and backup in Checkup: macOS updates, Time Machine, FileVault, the firewall, System Integrity Protection and Gatekeeper, each opening its place in System Settings.
  • Breaks have their own page: click the Breaks card in Tools to see your next two hours and pick a rhythm. A new switch lets breaks come even while a video plays.
  • Fixed: a break could be cancelled at the end of its countdown when a browser was playing video.
  • The Stop Charging At card in Tools opens Battery.
  • Calmer and quicker: pages swap with one cross-fade, the menu bar window and Find anything open at once, and Checkup's verdict is a ring of what was checked.
  • Lighter in the background: less CPU and less memory.
0.3.40
12 Nov 2026
  • Break rhythms: 20-20-20, Pomodoro, 50 and 10, Deep work, or your own. Work for 15 to 120 minutes, rest for 2 to 30, with a longer rest every few rests and eye breaks in between.
  • Sit up straight now rises gently from the bottom of the screen, and Blink grows out of the notch. Choose how often each comes.
  • A redesigned heads-up before each break.
  • Empty the Trash from Health › Storage: see how much is in it, kept up to date while the page is open, and empty it after one clear question, with a count as it goes. About twice as fast on external drives.
  • Clean Screen and Clean Keyboard: a black screen to wipe, or every key switched off while you clean the keyboard. In Tools, the menu bar window and Find anything.
  • Fixed: on some Macs, Full Disk Access looked switched off even after you turned it on.
0.3.33
10 Nov 2026
  • A whole new look, in light and dark: warm, glowing glass, gradient rings and charts, and one clear button on each screen.
  • A simpler window: six places, Now, Apps, History, Health, Tools and Settings, with tabs. ⌘1–⌘5 jump between them, and each remembers where you were.
  • Find anything: press ⌘K, type a few letters, and go to any page or run an action such as Take a Break or Charge to Full.
  • Checkup: is your Mac in good shape? One answer from the last week, with battery, storage, memory, heat, SSD and external drive health, and what starts with your Mac.
  • Stop charging at the level you choose, charge to full just this once, or schedule it. In Health, Tools and, if you like, the menu bar window.
  • Tools: internet and disk speed, Wi-Fi quality, Eject All, the Trash and a Mac report to paste into a support chat.
  • Breaks: gentle reminders to rest your eyes and sit up straight, held back while you're on a call or watching something.
  • Keep Awake: for a time, until the work is done, while an app is open, or with the lid closed while plugged in. One click in the menu bar, or ⌃⌥⌘K.
  • Flight Log: what your Mac did, day by day, from last night's sleep to crashes, macOS updates and time offline.
  • New alerts: a battery running hot, a lost connection, and a public IP that changed (for example when a VPN drops).
  • The menu bar window has one-click actions and Find anything, and Settings › Customize now sets up the menu bar, the notch and the menu bar window in one place.
  • History opens far faster.
