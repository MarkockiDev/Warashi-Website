Q&AQuestions & answers

Everything people ask about Warashi.

Can't find yours? Email adam@warashi.app.

What is Warashi?

A native menu bar app for Apple silicon Macs that measures CPU, memory, GPU, power, temperature, network, disk, battery and every app, keeps a year of history on your Mac, and turns it into answers: what slowed your Mac down, which app drained the battery, whether your Mac is in good shape, and what your next Mac needs. It also has a few tools you'll reach for: breaks for your eyes, Keep Awake, a charge limit and speed tests.

Is it available yet?

Now. It's a free public beta: download it from the home page and it keeps itself up to date.

Which Macs does it support?

Macs with Apple silicon (M1 and later) running macOS 26 or newer. Intel Macs aren't supported. The charge limit needs macOS 26.4 or later.

Is Warashi free?

Free during the public beta. Warashi 1.0 is a one-time purchase, with no subscription, for up to two Macs. When 1.0 ships, the beta stops and asks you to update and buy a licence; your history is kept.

What's new in this version?

A whole new look in light and dark, and a simpler window: six places (Now, Apps, History, Health, Tools and Settings) with tabs, and Find anything on ⌘K. New are Checkup, a charge limit with a one-off full charge and a schedule, Breaks, Keep Awake (even with the lid closed), the Flight Log, disk speed, Wi-Fi quality, Eject All, a Mac report, and alerts for a hot battery, a lost connection and a changed public IP. The changelog has every version.

Where did a page go?

Pages are grouped by what you want to do. Time × Cost and Developer are in Apps; Flight Log and Alerts in History; Checkup, Battery, Storage and the Upgrade Advisor in Health; Speed Test in Tools. Or press ⌘K and type its name. See Finding your way.

How is it different from Activity Monitor?

Activity Monitor shows this second. Warashi remembers: it records history, groups helpers under their app, shows percentiles and peaks, explains any moment, tells you what each app cost, checks your Mac's health and recommends your next Mac.

How is it different from iStat Menus or Stats?

Warashi is built around history and answers rather than many menu bar meters: one spirit in the menu bar, the notch and island, a window that tells your Mac's story, plain explanations of any moment, Power Flow, Checkup, Time × Cost and the Upgrade Advisor.

Does Warashi slow my Mac down?

No. In the background it uses about 0.2% of one core and around 70 MB of memory, and it shows you its own cost in Settings › General so you can check.

Can it limit how far my battery charges?

Yes. In Health › Battery, Checkup or Tools, switch on Stop charging at and choose a level from 80% to 100%; with macOS 26.7 or later and the optional System helper you can go down to 20%. Charge to Full charges to 100% just once, and Schedule does it at a time you choose. It needs macOS 26.4 or later. See Battery & charge limit.

Is a charge limit below 80% safe?

Below 80%, Warashi uses a setting Apple doesn't document, through the optional System helper, which accepts only one number between 20% and 75%. It can't harm the battery, but System Settings may show an unusual value, and a macOS update could stop it working. From 80% up, Warashi uses the same limit macOS itself offers.

What is Checkup?

The first tab in Health. It looks at the last week and says whether your Mac is in good shape, or what to look at: battery health, free space, memory, heat, and how much of your SSD's and external drives' rated life is used. It also lists everything that starts with your Mac and what it costs. See Checkup.

How do Breaks work? Do they watch my camera?

No. When a break is due, Warashi checks only whether your microphone or camera is in use, a video or presentation is keeping the screen awake, or a full-screen game is in front, and waits if so. It records none of it and needs no permissions. Breaks are off until you turn them on in Settings › Breaks. See Breaks.

Can my MacBook stay awake with the lid closed?

Yes, while it's plugged in: choose Stay Awake With the Lid Closed in the Keep Awake menu. It needs the optional System helper, lasts only for that session, and turns itself off if you unplug, if the Mac gets too hot, after 12 hours, or if Warashi stops. See Keep Awake.

What's in the Mac report?

Your Mac's model and chip, macOS version, last restart, memory, CPU, heat, disk space and SSD life, battery health and cycles, and the Warashi version. Nothing personal: no names, apps, file paths, serial numbers or history. It's copied to your clipboard for you to paste where you like.

Does my data leave my Mac?

Your history, app names and activity stay on your Mac, unless you choose to connect an AI app yourself. Warashi checks for updates, checks licences, and sends anonymous usage statistics (app version, macOS version, chip family and which features are used) to help improve it. A speed test contacts Apple only when you start one, and the Public IP alert asks Cloudflare for your address only while it's on. See Privacy & your data.

Does it use Apple Intelligence?

Only to word two things on your Mac: the Checkup verdict and the weekly summary's headline, on Macs where Apple's on-device model is available. The wording is checked against the facts, and nothing leaves your Mac. Without it, you get the same facts in plain words.

Does it need special permissions?

No permissions to start. It never asks for Screen Recording, Input Monitoring or Location. Full Disk Access is optional and only for Storage, which adds up sizes and never opens your files. Notifications are asked for only when you turn on an alert, and the optional System helper only when you turn on something that needs it.

What does the System helper do?

Exactly three things, only when you ask: it reads system processes such as WindowServer and kernel_task, sets a charge limit below 80%, and keeps your Mac awake with the lid closed on a lease that ends by itself. It takes no other commands and answers only Warashi. Turn it off in Settings › System to remove it. See The System helper.

Does it work without a notch?

Yes. On displays without a notch Warashi shows a small island at the top centre that works the same way. You can turn it off in Settings › Customize.

Can I choose what the menu bar, the notch and the menu bar window show?

Yes, all in Settings › Customize. Always Showing picks the number beside the spirit and the two beside the notch; Menu Bar Window and Open Notch show the real thing, live: press − or + to remove or add anything, and drag it where you want it. See Customize.

How much history does it keep?

Every 5 seconds for 24 hours, every minute for 30 days and every hour for 12 months, in a small local database. The Flight Log keeps 30 days.

Can I use it on two Macs?

Yes: a Warashi licence works on two Macs, and you can move it by deactivating it on one.

How do updates work?

Warashi checks for updates daily (you can turn that off) and only installs updates signed by its developer.

Does it have a light mode?

Yes. Settings › General › Appearance: follow your Mac, or always Light or Dark.

Can an AI app read my Mac's history?

Only if you add Warashi to one yourself. It's a read-only connection, and that app and its service then receive app names and usage history when they ask, never activity records or file paths.

Can I get my data out?

Yes. Settings › General › Privacy › Export as CSV writes everything Warashi has recorded to a folder you choose.

Does it need Full Disk Access to show storage?

No, it's optional. Without it, Storage measures everything macOS doesn't protect and leaves the rest alone, so you never get a stream of permission prompts. Switch it on once, in the welcome tour or on the Storage page, to include Desktop, Documents, Downloads and app data. Warashi never deletes anything.

Can it test my internet and disk speed?

Yes. Speed Test uses Apple's own test when you press the button and shows your speed live; a test can use 2 GB of data or more on a fast connection, so avoid it on a hotspot. Disk Speed, in Tools, measures how fast your disk reads and writes in about six seconds and leaves nothing behind.

How do I uninstall it?

Turn off the System helper first if you enabled it (Settings › System), quit Warashi (⋯ › Quit Warashi in the menu bar window), then move Warashi to the Bin. Its history lives in ~/Library/Application Support/Warashi if you want to delete that too.
