Q&AQuestions & answers

Everything people ask about Warashi.

Can't find yours? Email adam@warashi.app.

What is Warashi?

A native menu bar app for Apple silicon Macs that measures CPU, memory, GPU, power, temperature, network, disk, battery and every app, keeps a year of history on your Mac, and turns it into answers: what slowed your Mac down, which app drained the battery, whether your Mac is in good shape, and what your next Mac needs. It also has a few tools you'll reach for: breaks for your eyes, Keep Awake, a charge limit and speed tests.

Is it available yet?

Now. It's a free public beta: download it from the home page and it keeps itself up to date.

Which Macs does it support?

Macs with Apple silicon (M1 and later) running macOS 26 or newer. Intel Macs aren't supported. The charge limit needs macOS 26.4 or later.

Is Warashi free?

Free during the public beta. Warashi 1.0 is a one-time purchase, with no subscription, for up to two Macs. When 1.0 ships, the beta stops and asks you to update and buy a licence; your history is kept.

What's new in this version?

Warashi 0.3.61 (21 November 2026) fixes the break countdown, which could vanish and start again from 0:15. 0.3.60 stopped Keychain prompts out of the blue: macOS asks about Claude Code’s or Cursor’s sign-in only when you turn a usage limit on. 0.3.59 brought a new welcome tour that asks for each permission where its feature is shown. The changelog has every version.

Where did a page go?

Pages are grouped by what you want to do. Time × Cost and Developer are in Apps; Flight Log and Alerts in History; Checkup, Battery, Storage and the Upgrade Advisor in Health; Speed Test in Tools. Or press ⌘K and type its name. See Finding your way.

Where is the Help?

On this website, in all seven languages. In Warashi, choose Warashi Help in the Help menu (⌘?), in Settings › General or in the menu bar window’s ⋯ menu: each opens these pages in the language Warashi uses. See Help.

How is it different from Activity Monitor?

Activity Monitor shows this second. Warashi remembers: it records history, groups helpers under their app, shows percentiles and peaks, explains any moment, tells you what each app cost, checks your Mac's health and recommends your next Mac.

How is it different from iStat Menus or Stats?

Warashi is built around history and answers rather than many menu bar meters: one spirit in the menu bar, the notch and island, a window that tells your Mac's story, plain explanations of any moment, Power Flow, Checkup, Time × Cost and the Upgrade Advisor.

Does Warashi slow my Mac down?

No. In the background it uses about 0.2% of one core and around 70 MB of memory, and it shows you its own cost in Settings › General so you can check.

Can it limit how far my battery charges?

Yes. In Health › Battery, Checkup or Tools, switch on Stop charging at and choose a level from 80% to 100%; with macOS 26.7 or later and the optional System helper you can go down to 20%. Charge to Full charges to 100% just once, and Schedule does it at a time you choose. It needs macOS 26.4 or later. See Battery & charge limit.

Is a charge limit below 80% safe?

Below 80%, Warashi uses a setting Apple doesn't document, through the optional System helper, which accepts only one number between 20% and 75%. It can't harm the battery, but System Settings may show an unusual value, and a macOS update could stop it working. From 80% up, Warashi uses the same limit macOS itself offers.

Can my Mac run on its battery while it's plugged in?

Yes, with the optional System helper. In Health › Battery or Checkup, press Discharge to 50%, or pick 80, 70, 60, 50, 40 or 30% from its arrow. Your Mac runs on the battery with the charger still plugged in, then charging takes over again at that level. It stops by itself if you unplug, the Mac sleeps or gets hot, the battery runs low, or Warashi quits. You don't need it to come down to your charge limit: macOS does that by itself. See Battery & charge limit.

What is Checkup?

The first tab in Health. It looks at the last week and says whether your Mac is in good shape, or what to look at: battery health, free space, memory, heat, and how much of your SSD's and external drives' rated life is used. It also lists everything that starts with your Mac and what it costs. See Checkup.

How do Breaks work? Do they watch my camera?

No. When a break is due, Warashi checks only whether your microphone or camera is in use, a video or presentation is keeping the screen awake, or a full-screen game is in front, and waits if so. It records none of it and needs no permissions. Breaks are off until you turn them on in Settings › Breaks. See Breaks.

Why is my Mac slow?

Open Health › Checkup, press Feeling Slow? and keep doing what felt slow. Warashi watches for ten seconds, compares what it sees with your Mac's usual week, and names the most likely cause: heat, memory, one busy app, macOS's own work such as Spotlight indexing, or a nearly full disk. It gives the numbers and one thing to do. See Checkup.

Does Warashi check my Mac's security?

Yes, and only looks. Checkup shows whether a macOS update is waiting, when Time Machine last backed up, and whether FileVault, the firewall, System Integrity Protection and Gatekeeper are on. It asks macOS's own tools when Checkup opens, never in the background, and changes nothing. Each tile opens its place in System Settings. See Checkup.

Can I set my own break rhythm, like Pomodoro?

Yes. In Tools › Breaks, Your rhythm offers 20-20-20, Pomodoro (25 minutes of work, 5 of rest, 15 after every fourth), 50 and 10, and Deep work (90 and 20). Or set your own: how long you work, how long you rest, and a longer rest every few. Short eye breaks can run in between. See Breaks.

Can Warashi empty my Trash?

Yes, when you ask. The Trash card in Health › Storage shows how much is in it, including on connected drives, and Empty Trash… deletes it for good after asking you first. It needs Full Disk Access, because macOS guards the Trash. If something stays because only an administrator can delete it, such as an app an installer put in Applications, Warashi asks Finder to finish, and Finder asks for your password. See Storage.

Can Warashi remind me when the Trash gets big?

Yes. Turn on Trash getting big in Settings › Alerts and choose 1, 2, 5 or 10 GB. When the Trash passes it, a card drops from Warashi's menu bar item with its size: Empty Trash… asks once, then deletes it for good, and Ignore keeps quiet until it grows by that much again, or for a week. It needs Full Disk Access. See Alerts.

Why does Warashi ask to control Finder?

Only when emptying the Trash leaves something behind that only an administrator can delete, such as an app an installer put in Applications. Warashi then asks Finder to empty the rest, and Finder asks for your password, as when you empty the Trash yourself. macOS asks once whether Warashi may control Finder, and Warashi uses it for nothing else. You can turn it off in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation.

How do I clean my screen or keyboard without pressing anything?

Use Clean Screen or Clean Keyboard in Tools, the menu bar window's ⋯ menu, or Find anything. Clean Screen turns every display black, and Esc finishes. Clean Keyboard switches every key off while you wipe; hold Esc for two seconds to finish. Neither needs a permission, and both end by themselves after two minutes. See Tools.

What is Soft Focus?

A tool that quiets your screen: every window except the one you’re working in softens into a blur, so only your work stays sharp. Turn it on from Tools or with ⌃⌥⌘F, or with a shake of the pointer once you switch that on. It needs no permission: macOS does the blurring, and Warashi only knows where the window you’re using is, never what’s in it. See Soft Focus.

Can my MacBook stay awake with the lid closed?

Yes, while it's plugged in: choose Stay Awake With the Lid Closed in the Keep Awake menu. It needs the optional System helper, lasts only for that session, and turns itself off if you unplug, if the Mac gets too hot, after 12 hours, or if Warashi stops. See Keep Awake.

What's in the Mac report?

Your Mac's model and chip, macOS version, last restart, memory, CPU, heat, disk space and SSD life, battery health and cycles, and the Warashi version. Nothing personal: no names, apps, file paths, serial numbers or history. It's copied to your clipboard for you to paste where you like.

Does my data leave my Mac?

Your history, app names and activity stay on your Mac, unless you choose to connect an AI app yourself. Warashi checks for updates, checks licences, and sends anonymous usage statistics (app version, macOS version, chip family and which features are used) to help improve it. A speed test contacts Apple only when you start one, the Public IP alert asks Cloudflare for your address only while it's on, and Claude's and Cursor's usage limits are asked of Anthropic and Cursor only while you show them. See Privacy & your data.

Does it use Apple Intelligence?

Only to word two things on your Mac: the Checkup verdict and the weekly summary's headline, on Macs where Apple's on-device model is available. The wording is checked against the facts, and nothing leaves your Mac. Without it, you get the same facts in plain words.

Does it need special permissions?

No permissions to start. It never asks for Screen Recording, Input Monitoring or Location. Full Disk Access is optional and only for Storage, which adds up sizes and never opens your files. Notifications are asked for only when you turn on an alert, and the optional System helper only when you turn on something that needs it. Emptying the Trash may ask, once, to let Warashi control Finder, for items that need your password. Soft Focus needs no permission at all.

What does the System helper do?

Exactly four things, only when you ask: it reads system processes such as WindowServer and kernel_task, sets a charge limit below 80%, keeps your Mac awake with the lid closed, and pauses the charger so your Mac runs on its battery while plugged in. The last two run on short leases that end by themselves. It takes no other commands and answers only Warashi. Turn it off in Settings › System to remove it. See The System helper.

Does it work without a notch?

Yes. On displays without a notch Warashi shows a small island at the top centre that works the same way. You can turn it off in Settings › Customize.

I use another notch app. Which one shows?

Both apps watch the notch, so pointing at it can open both. Warashi’s notch stays on top and answers the pointer, and Settings › Customize › Always Showing names the other app. To see only Warashi, turn off the notch in the other app. See Notch & island.

Can I choose what the menu bar, the notch and the menu bar window show?

Yes, all in Settings › Customize. Always Showing picks the number beside the spirit and the two beside the notch; Menu Bar Window and Open Notch show the real thing, live: press − or + to remove or add anything, and drag it where you want it. See Customize.

Can I see my battery level in the menu bar?

Yes. In Settings › Customize › Beside the spirit, choose Battery: a battery filled to its level, with a bolt while charging, a pause while it's held at your charge limit, and the percentage. Battery time left shows the time instead. The same list offers your coding agent's limit, Keep Awake's time left, the next break, and VPN with download speed. See The menu bar.

How much history does it keep?

Every 5 seconds for 24 hours, every minute for 30 days and every hour for 12 months, in a small local database. The Flight Log keeps 30 days.

Can I use it on two Macs?

Yes: a Warashi licence works on two Macs, and you can move it by deactivating it on one.

How do updates work?

Warashi checks for updates daily (you can turn that off) and only installs updates signed by its developer.

Does it have a light mode?

Yes. Settings › General › Appearance: follow your Mac, or always Light or Dark.

Can an AI app read my Mac's history?

Only if you add Warashi to one yourself. It's a read-only connection, and that app and its service then receive app names and usage history when they ask, never activity records or file paths.

Does Warashi work with Claude Code, Codex and Cursor?

Yes, once you turn on Watch Claude Code, Codex and Cursor in Settings › Coding Agents. Apps › Developer then shows each session and what it's doing, with the 5-hour and weekly limits and when they reset. Warashi can tell you, in a notification and the notch, when an agent needs you or has finished, keep your Mac awake while one works, and write long sessions in the Flight Log. See Coding agents.

Does Warashi read my code or prompts?

No. It reads only each session's state and its limits from the agents' own files, and only the dates of Cursor's chat files: never your code, prompts or conversations. It shows a project folder's name, never its path. See Privacy & your data.

How do I connect Claude's usage limit?

Claude Code doesn't keep its limit on your Mac, so Warashi asks Anthropic for it with the sign-in Claude Code already has. Turn on Show Claude's usage limit in Settings › Coding Agents and choose Always Allow when macOS asks about the Keychain. If you're not signed in, Sign In opens Claude Code's own sign-in in Terminal. Warashi never stores the sign-in, and renews a stale one by asking Claude Code itself. See Coding agents.

Can I get my data out?

Yes. Settings › General › Privacy › Export as CSV writes everything Warashi has recorded to a folder you choose.

Does it need Full Disk Access to show storage?

No, it's optional. Without it, Storage measures everything macOS doesn't protect and leaves the rest alone, so you never get a stream of permission prompts. Switch it on once, in the welcome tour or on the Storage page, to include Desktop, Documents, Downloads and app data. Warashi never deletes anything.

Can Warashi free up space from Xcode, Docker and other developer tools?

Yes. Health › Storage › Developer finds what developer tools leave behind: Xcode's DerivedData, old simulators and device support, package caches, and Docker's unused images. Each item says what it is and whether it comes back by itself; you choose what goes, and it goes to the Trash (old simulators and Docker are removed by their own tools after you say yes). Checkup can also clean up Homebrew. See Storage.

Can it test my internet and disk speed?

Yes. Speed Test uses Apple's own test when you press the button and shows your speed live; a test can use 2 GB of data or more on a fast connection, so avoid it on a hotspot. Disk Speed, in Tools, measures how fast your disk reads and writes in about six seconds and leaves nothing behind.

How do I uninstall it?

Turn off the System helper first if you enabled it (Settings › System), quit Warashi (⋯ › Quit Warashi in the menu bar window), then move Warashi to the Bin. Its history lives in ~/Library/Application Support/Warashi if you want to delete that too.

Why does Warashi appear in its own Apps list?

Because it measures itself like any other app and shows the result, marked “this window”. While its window is open it redraws live numbers every second, so it costs a few percent of one core; closed, it drops to a fraction of a percent. It never appears in the busiest-apps cards or the menu bar window's rankings.

How much CPU and memory does Warashi use in the background?

About 0.1 to 0.3% of one core and 30 to 60 MB with the window closed, measured on an M5 MacBook. While the screens sleep or the Mac is locked it stops drawing and takes a reading every 30 seconds instead of every 5. Settings › General shows Warashi's own cost, measured the same way as every other app.

Why did my battery charge past the limit I set?

Another charge manager (AlDente, Battery Toolkit and the like) can override the limit, for example during a calibration. Warashi warns on the Battery page when it sees one, and the Flight Log notes each time the battery went past your limit and which app was managing charging. Keep one charge manager, not two.

The System helper stopped working after I reinstalled. What happened?

If the old Warashi was removed before the new one was copied in, macOS forgets the helper's approval but keeps its old job, and the helper can't start until the Mac restarts. Restart, then turn Show system processes off and on in Settings › System if it's still red. Updating from Settings › Updates, or dragging the new app over the old one, avoids it.

Is it safe to remove apps with Warashi?

Yes. Nothing is deleted: every app and leftover goes to the Trash, one item at a time, after you've seen each one, and you can put anything back until you empty the Trash. Warashi only lists leftovers it can tie to the app by its bundle identifier or name, and leaves shared folders alone.

Why don't breaks come during a call?

Settings › Breaks › Wait during calls and meetings holds every break and nudge while your microphone or camera is in use, so a reminder never lands mid-sentence. Turn it off to be reminded anyway. Breaks also pause outside your work hours and during a Focus such as Do Not Disturb, if you've turned those on.

My Mac looked asleep when I came back, even with Keep Awake on. Why?

Most of the time the Mac was awake and only the screen had turned off and locked, which macOS does on its own. Check the Flight Log: a sleep entry means the Mac really slept, none means only the display did. Keep Awake with the lid closed also needs the charger and the System helper.

Does Warashi work on an external display, or a Mac without a notch?

Yes. On displays without a notch the same two readings sit on a small island at the top of the screen, which you can turn off per display in Settings › Customize. Everything else, the menu bar item, the menu bar window and the main window, is the same on every Mac.
