Q&AQuestions & answers

Everything people ask about Warashi.

Can't find yours? Email adam@warashi.app.

What is Warashi?

A native menu bar app for Apple silicon Macs that measures CPU, memory, GPU, power, temperature, network, disk, battery and every app, keeps a year of history on your Mac, and turns it into answers: what slowed your Mac down, which app drained the battery, and what your next Mac needs.

Is it available yet?

Now. It's a free public beta: download it from the home page and it keeps itself up to date.

Which Macs does it support?

Macs with Apple silicon (M1 and later) running macOS 26 or newer. Intel Macs aren't supported.

Is Warashi free?

Free during the public beta. Warashi 1.0 is a one-time purchase, with no subscription, for up to two Macs. When 1.0 ships, the beta stops and asks you to update and buy a licence; your history is kept.

How is it different from Activity Monitor?

Activity Monitor shows this second. Warashi remembers: it records history, groups helpers under their app, shows percentiles and peaks, tells you what each app cost and recommends your next Mac.

How is it different from iStat Menus or Stats?

Warashi is built around history and answers rather than many menu bar meters: one spirit in the menu bar, the notch and island, a window that tells your Mac's story, plain explanations of any moment, Power Flow, Time × Cost and the Upgrade Advisor.

Does Warashi slow my Mac down?

No. It uses about 0.16% of one core and around 25 MB of memory in the background, and it shows you its own cost so you can check.

Does my data leave my Mac?

Your history, app names and activity stay on your Mac, unless you choose to connect an AI app yourself. Warashi checks for updates, checks licences, and sends anonymous usage statistics (app version, macOS version, chip family and which features are used) to help improve it. See Privacy & your data.

Does it need special permissions?

No permissions to start. It never asks for Screen Recording, Input Monitoring or Full Disk Access. Notifications are asked for only when you turn on an alert, and the optional system-process helper only when you turn it on.

Can it show WindowServer and kernel_task?

Yes, with the optional helper in Settings › System. It only reads process counters and you can remove it any time.

Does it work without a notch?

Yes. On displays without a notch Warashi shows a small island at the top centre that works the same way. You can turn it off.

How much history does it keep?

Every 5 seconds for 24 hours, every minute for 30 days and every hour for 12 months, in a small local database.

Can I use it on two Macs?

Yes: a Warashi licence works on two Macs, and you can move it by deactivating it on one.

How do updates work?

Warashi checks for updates daily (you can turn that off) and only installs updates signed by its developer.

What's new in Warashi?

Power Flow, a Developer section for ports and AI agents, plain explanations of any moment in History, a weekly summary, forecast alerts, connected devices, a sleep report, light mode and CSV export. The changelog has every version.

Does it have a light mode?

Yes. Settings › General › Appearance: follow your Mac, or always Light or Dark.

Can it limit how far my battery charges?

No. Charge limiting means writing to the battery controller as root, and Warashi only reads. Power Flow does show when charging is being held.

Can an AI app read my Mac's history?

Only if you add Warashi to one yourself. It's a read-only connection, and that app and its service then receive app names and usage history when they ask, never activity records or file paths.

Can I get my data out?

Yes. Settings › General › Privacy › Export as CSV writes everything Warashi has recorded to a folder you choose.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit Warashi, turn off the system-process helper first if you enabled it, then move Warashi to the Bin. Its history lives in ~/Library/Application Support/Warashi if you want to delete that too.
