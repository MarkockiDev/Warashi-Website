How to uninstall apps on a Mac completely, leftovers included

Where Mac apps keep their files, how to find what a deleted app left in your Library, and how to clear it without deleting anything you'll miss.

5 min read · Updated 14 November 2026

Dragging an app to the Trash removes the app and leaves its baggage behind: caches, preferences, saved state, logs, sometimes gigabytes in ~/Library. Here is where apps keep their things on a Mac, how to find what a removed app left, and how to clear it without deleting anything you'll miss.

Where apps keep their files

Apps store almost everything under your user Library, which Finder hides. Hold ⌥ while opening the Finder's Go menu to see it. The usual places, named after the app's bundle identifier (such as com.company.app):

  • ~/Library/Application Support/: the app's own data, often the biggest.
  • ~/Library/Caches/: safe to remove; the app rebuilds them.
  • ~/Library/Preferences/: settings, small.
  • ~/Library/Containers/ and Group Containers/: sandboxed apps' data.
  • ~/Library/Saved Application State/, Logs/, HTTPStorages/, WebKit/: small, safe to remove.
  • ~/Library/LaunchAgents/: helpers that start at login; remove the app's so it stops trying to run.

The order that doesn't go wrong

  1. Quit the app, and its menu bar helper if it has one.
  2. Use the app's own uninstaller if it ships one (many developer tools and anti-virus apps do).
  3. Move the app to the Trash from Applications. If macOS asks for your password, the app installed a helper; that's normal.
  4. Find the leftovers by searching each folder above for the app's name and bundle identifier.
  5. Move them to the Trash too, and leave the Trash alone for a few days before emptying it, in case something turns out to be shared with another app.

What not to delete

Anything in Group Containers that several apps from the same developer share, anything under /Library (the system-wide one) you don't recognise, and the iCloud Drive folders of apps whose documents you still want. When in doubt, leave it: a leftover costs disk space, a wrong deletion costs data.

Leftovers of apps you removed long ago

Every Mac accumulates folders from apps that are gone: a trial you forgot, a tool you replaced. They're easy to spot once you know the pattern: a folder in Application Support or Containers whose bundle identifier matches no app in Applications. These are the safest things on the disk to clear.
